01

Level One · Self-paced · Day 1

Choose Your Player

Who's logging in, and what are they trying to do?

Before you build a single thing in Qualtrics, you've got a choice to make: who's playing? An admin? A frontline manager? An exec who only logs in for the dashboard? Each one needs a different setup, and that setup gets harder to fix later. Better to make the call now.

Spend 20 minutes reading. Spend 20 minutes building your player cards. Bring those cards to your team — they're the start of every conversation about who gets access to what.

What you leave with

  • Two to four named players, each with a defined goal
  • A unique identifier strategy for both customers and employees
  • A printable player card set for internal alignment

Level Two · Self-paced · Day 3

Pick Your Loadout

The data and identifiers you carry into every project.

Every Qualtrics project carries the same core data with it — fields like region, role, department, customer ID. We call that embedded data, and your loadout is the list of fields you're going to standardize across every project from here on out.

This is the level customers regret skipping in year two. Pick your fields well now and every dashboard you build later just works. Pick badly and you'll be cleaning up in 18 months.

What you leave with

  • A defined embedded data field list with rationale for each one
  • Confirmed unique identifiers for customers and employees
  • A pressure-tested loadout sheet ready for the live levels

Level Three · Live webinar · Day 5

Live

Level Up

Build your first survey live with the cohort.

No watching. No demos. You're in your own Qualtrics instance, building a project intake survey with a cohort of other new customers. The facilitator builds alongside you. When you get stuck — and you will — you raise your hand and we figure it out together. That's the whole point.

Bring your player cards from Level 01 and your loadout sheet from Level 02. You'll need both. Cameras on if you can.

What the cohort leaves with

  • A working project intake survey live in their own instance
  • Usage tags configured and tested
  • Confidence that the work from Levels 01 and 02 shows up in real fields
  • A side quest: test the survey with one teammate before Level 04

04

Level Four · Live webinar · Day 8

Live

Power Up

Connect your survey to a working ticket flow.

Your intake survey collects requests. Now we close the loop — every new request creates a ticket, the ticket carries decision and status fields, and the requestor gets a notification when something changes. By the end, you'll have an end-to-end flow that runs on its own.

We'll also walk through the four integration patterns you'll meet again and again: connectors, public APIs, flat files, and event-driven workflows. Honest about where each one breaks.

What the cohort leaves with

  • A working ticket flow tied to their intake survey
  • Practical fluency in the four integration patterns
  • An end-to-end loop: request → ticket → status → notification
  • A side quest: share the survey with stakeholders before Level 05

05

Level Five · Community · Day 10

Join the Party

Show your work. Find your next lab.

This last level is short by design. Two things to do — both quick, both useful.

First, introduce yourself to the community. Three sentences: who you are, what you do, and the one thing you're going to use Qualtrics for in the next 90 days. Naming it forces clarity. The cohort sees who's building what. Sometimes that's where the best questions come from.

Second, browse the lab catalog and sign up for at least two more. We'll point you at the ones that match the players and use cases you defined back in Level 01.

What you leave with

  • A public commitment to a 90-day outcome
  • At least two more labs on the calendar
  • Visibility into what other new customers are building
  • An L0 completion mark on your community profile
