Beat 01 / Read

What embedded data actually is.

Six minutes. Three blocks. Each one ends with the trap people fall into. Keep those traps in mind when you build your loadout in Beat 2.

📦

What it is

Embedded data are context fields that travel with every survey response. They're not survey questions — the respondent never sees them. They're metadata: who answered, from which team, in which region, at what customer tier.

A response without embedded data is a signal without context. You know someone is unhappy. You don't know who, where, or why it matters to fix.

Common trap Adding embedded data after launch. The fields only populate for responses collected after you add them. Everything before is blank forever.
🎯

What belongs in it

Anything you'll use to slice data in a dashboard. Region, department, role, team size, customer tier, account type, tenure band. If you'll filter by it, it belongs. If you won't, it's noise.

The right number of fields is the minimum set that answers your five most important questions. Not every field your data warehouse has. Not every field anyone might want someday.

Common trap The "we might need it" fields. They bloat your directory, never get used, and make the real fields harder to find. Cut them now.
🔑

Unique identifiers

Every contact in your directory needs one field that makes them uniquely identifiable — an email, an employee ID, a CRM customer ID. This is how Qualtrics links responses across projects over time.

You need two: one for customers, one for employees. They don't have to be the same type. They do have to be stable — a field that changes when someone gets promoted will break your history.

Common trap Using name or display name as the identifier. Names change, duplicate, and vary by system. Always use a system-generated ID.

Beat 02 / Build

Build your loadout.

🔒 Finish Beat 1 to unlock the builder.

Name your embedded data fields, write a one-line rationale for each one, then set your unique identifiers. Minimum five fields. Cut anything you can't justify.

The discipline

Every field you add here gets inherited by every project. That's the power. It's also the risk. Name things badly and you'll be cleaning up for years.

  • Use snake_case — no spaces, no capitals, no special characters
  • Rationale = one sentence. If you can't explain it, cut it
  • Minimum 5 fields. Maximum 20. Over 20 and you're over-engineering
  • Customer and employee identifiers are required, not optional

Beat 03 / Test

Pressure test.

🔒 Add at least 5 fields with rationale and both identifiers to unlock.

Three scenarios. Each one is a real thing that happens. For each one, does your loadout handle it — or does it fall over? Any gap you surface here is easier to fix now than after Level 03.

  1. 01

    A new project gets requested.

    Six months from now, a team you haven't built for yet needs a new survey. They want their own slice of the dashboard.

    Does your loadout include the fields that will segment this new team's responses from everyone else's?

    What field would you add or change? One sentence.

    ✓ Answered
  2. 02

    A leader changes.

    A regional VP moves to a new role. Their replacement covers a slightly different geography. Your dashboard needs to update automatically.

    Does your loadout use fields that reflect org structure without being tied to specific people's names?

    What would you change? One sentence.

    ✓ Answered
  3. 03

    A region splits.

    "North America" becomes "US East," "US West," and "Canada." Six months of data is filed under the old structure. You need to report on the new structure going forward.

    Does your region field have enough granularity to support a split like this — or would a split require remapping all your historical data?

    How would you make the field more resilient?

    ✓ Answered

What you do with this

Walk the loadout across to whoever owns your data systems. Make sure the fields you named here can actually be sourced from your HR system or CRM. If a field can't be sourced, it doesn't belong in your loadout — it becomes guess-work in the dashboard.
